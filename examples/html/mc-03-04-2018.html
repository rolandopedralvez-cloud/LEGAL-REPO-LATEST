Guidelines on Electronic Billing (E-Billing)

Type
regulation
Status
active
Effective date
2018-03-04
Source/Authority
RA 7394 (Consumer Act); RA 7925; RA 8792 (E-Commerce Act); CA 146

Memorandum Circular No. 03-04-2018 allows public telecommunications entities (PTEs) to offer electronic billing to subscribers, giving subscribers the choice between electronic and paper bills and setting minimum consumer-protection safeguards for the practice.

Framework for offering e-billing

PTEs may offer to send bills electronically, but must first adopt their own e-billing rules and procedures, publish them in a newspaper of general circulation, and submit them to NTC; NTC may suspend implementation of any PTE's procedures found inconsistent with existing law. This builds on MC 05-06-2007's existing requirement that subscribers only be charged per agreed rates and receive 30 days' advance notice of service-affecting changes.

Subscriber rights and protections

Every subscriber has the option to choose electronic bills over paper bills, and vice versa, at any time, subject to the provider's notice procedures; new subscribers choose at contract signing, while existing subscribers may switch on notice from the provider. Subscribers electing paper bills must receive them free of any charge, including delivery fees. Electronic statements of account must be sent within 30 days of the end of each monthly billing cycle to the subscriber's chosen email or other agreed medium.

Enforcement and effectivity

PTEs found violating the Circular are dealt with under existing law. It took effect fifteen (15) days after publication in a newspaper of general circulation and filing of three certified true copies with the UP Law Center.
