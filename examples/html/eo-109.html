Executive Orders

Executive Order No. 109 — Policy to Improve the Provision of Local Exchange Carrier Service

Status
Active
Signed
July 12, 1993
Signed By
President Fidel V. Ramos, by Executive Secretary Teofisto T. Guingona, Jr.

Finding that basic local exchange service is fundamental to universal access, and that the industry's profits mainly come from toll and international long-distance services rather than local exchange, this Order directs new policy to promote orderly development, satisfy unserved demand for telephones, and encourage healthy competition.

Sections 1–2 — Definitions and Objective

Defines Basic Telecommunications Service, Cost-based and Value-based pricing, Local Exchange Carrier Service, and Universal Access. The Order's objective is to improve local exchange service provision in unserved and underserved areas as defined by NTC, promoting universal access to basic telecommunications service.

Sections 3–8 — Cross-Subsidy and Service Packaging

Local exchange service continues to be cross-subsidized by other telecommunications services within the same company until universal access is achieved. Authorized international gateway operators must provide local exchange service in unserved and underserved areas, including Metro Manila, within three years of authorization, subject to minimum line-provisioning guidelines: at least 300 local exchange lines per international switch termination, one rural exchange line for every ten urban lines, and credit toward the obligation for establishing rural barangay Public Calling Offices. Subsidiaries of a carrier operating an international gateway may not operate another gateway consistent with EO 59 (1993). Gateway operators may also be authorized to provide Cellular Mobile Telephone System service as a further subsidy source, and other non-basic service providers may likewise be required to provide local exchange service.

Sections 11–18 — Interconnection, Reporting, Transitory, and Penalties

All telecommunications networks must interconnect on a non-discriminatory basis per EO 59 and its implementing guidelines. Internal subsidy flows must be made explicit in financial reporting. NTC was directed to issue implementing rules within 30 days. Existing service providers were given five years to comply. Violations carry the same penalties as EO 59 violations. Effective immediately upon issuance, Manila, July 12, 1993.
