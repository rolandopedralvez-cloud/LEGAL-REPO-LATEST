Rules on the Measurement of Fixed Broadband/Internet Access Service

Type
regulation
Status
active
Effective date
2015-07-08
Source/Authority
RA 7925; EO 546, s. 1979

Memorandum Circular No. 07-08-2015 establishes disclosure obligations for fixed broadband/internet service providers and a Commission-run measurement regime covering data rate, latency, jitter, and packet loss, with results published monthly.

Definitions and disclosure obligations

The Circular defines broadband (per ITU, at least 256 kbps), fixed wireless, cable, DSL and FTTH broadband, packet, jitter, latency, packet loss, and end-to-end delay. ISPs must keep subscribers informed of the service being offered (through flyers, brochures, and advertisements), disclose average downstream/upstream data rates per administrative area, and include service rates in advertisements and service agreements. Data-volume-capped plans must automatically notify subscribers at 80%, 85%, 90%, 95%, and 100% of consumption, informing them that continued use after 100% incurs normal rates; ISPs with fewer than 5,000 subscribers may instead use a 24/7 help-desk hotline for this purpose.

Measurement methodology

The Commission conducts monitoring of downstream/upstream data rate, latency, jitter, and packet loss, measured at technology-specific points (e.g. the DSL station protector, the FTTH ONU's 'IN' port, the fixed-wireless modem's 'IN' port, or the cable modem's LAN port) using standard, malware-free, commercially available equipment with no other applications running. Measurements are taken at a mix of locations agreed with ISPs and consumer groups ('known') and Commission-selected ('unknown', disclosed within 2 days after testing), against servers within the ISP's LAN, within the national network, and outside the Philippines, during both peak and off-peak hours at least twice a week, excluding adverse weather conditions, using an official Commission-prescribed and publicly downloadable measurement tool.

Publication and other rules

Results are posted on the Commission's website within five days of each month's end, released to media, averaged per ISP, and made available to the public on request, specifying the measurement points, date/time, and data plans involved; each ISP is given an opportunity to respond before publication. Providers offering a Committed Information Rate (CIR) must still comply with Memorandum Circular No. 12-19-2004, only franchised and authorized PTEs may provide broadband/internet networks, and Memorandum Order No. 07-07-2011 remains in effect. Any inconsistent issuance is deemed repealed or amended.
