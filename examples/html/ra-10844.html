Republic Act No. 10844 — An Act Creating the Department of Information and Communications Technology, Defining Its Powers and Functions, Appropriating Funds Therefor, and for Other Purposes

Status
Active
Enacted
July 27, 2015, Sixteenth Congress
Short Title
Department of Information and Communications Technology Act of 2015

Sections 1–3 — Short Title, Declaration of Policy, Definitions

Declares State policy on ICT infrastructure as an instrument of good governance and global competitiveness, universal ICT access, convergence, sector development, digital literacy, privacy, cybersecurity, and consumer protection. Defines ICT, Convergence, and related terms.

Sections 4–6 — Creation, Mandate, and Powers

Creates the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) as the primary policy, planning, coordinating, implementing, and administrative entity of the Executive Branch for national ICT development. DICT's powers span policy and planning, ICT infrastructure development, cybersecurity, e-government, and industry promotion.

Attached Agencies

The National Telecommunications Commission (NTC), the National Privacy Commission, and the Cybercrime Investigation and Coordination Center are attached to DICT for policy and program coordination, continuing to operate under their own charters and laws insofar as not inconsistent with this Act. Cybersecurity functions — including the National Cybersecurity Plan and National Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT) — are transferred to DICT. This supersedes the earlier chain of NTC's attachment history under EO 546 and EO 47.
