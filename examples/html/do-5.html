Department Order No. 5, Series of 1948 — Rules and Regulations Governing Commercial Radio Operators

Status
Amended — foundational licensing framework, superseded in parts by later NTC issuances but still cited as historical basis
Effective Date
September 22, 1948
Legal Basis
Act No. 3846 (Radio Control Law), as amended by Commonwealth Acts Nos. 365 and 571
Signed By
Cornelio Balmaceda, Secretary of Commerce and Industry

Issued pursuant to the Radio Control Law, this Order establishes the licensing framework for commercial radio operators and repeals all conflicting prior rules, effective immediately upon issuance.

Licensing Requirement

Only a licensed radio operator may operate a radio station requiring a license, including remote-controlled facilities.

License Classes

Three categories of operator license are established — radiotelephone, radiotelegraph, and radioteletype — each subdivided into three classes, with each class authorizing operation of specific station types, emission types, and power limitations.

Applicant Qualifications

Applicants must be Philippine citizens (subject to exceptions), at least eighteen years old, of good moral character, high school graduates, and either graduates of a recognized radio school or government radio operators with at least one year of experience.

Examinations and Renewal

Applicants undergo written examinations covering basic law, radio theory and practice, and technical content specific to the license class sought. Operators with at least ninety days of satisfactory service within the preceding six months may renew their license without retaking the examination.

Fees

Examination fees range from Three to Twelve Pesos; annual license fees range from Three to Twelve Pesos, depending on license class.

Suspension, Revocation, and Penalties

Licenses may be suspended or revoked for regulatory violations, false statements, non-compliance with license conditions, or intentional damage to equipment. Violations of this Order are punishable by a fine of up to Two Thousand Pesos, imprisonment of up to two years, or both.
