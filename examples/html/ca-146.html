Foundational Laws

Commonwealth Act No. 146 — The Public Service Act

Status
Amended, still in force — foundational law underlying NTC's certificate-of-public-convenience authority over telecommunications carriers
Approved
November 7, 1936
Amended By
Presidential Decree No. 1; EO 546; Republic Acts 178, 723, 2031, 2677, 3792

Definitions

"Public service" is defined broadly to include every entity operating for compensation any common carrier, railroad, street railway, traction railway, subway, motor vehicle, and other public conveyance or infrastructure, as well as telecommunications and water systems. "Person" includes every individual, co-partnership, joint-stock company, or corporation, whether domestic or foreign, as well as covered government entities.

Chapter I — The Public Service Commission

The Act creates the Public Service Commission under the Department of Justice, composed of a Public Service Commissioner and five Associate Commissioners. Commissioners must be natural-born Philippine citizens, at least thirty years old, members of the Bar with at least five years of legal practice or relevant government service, appointed by the President with the consent of the Commission on Appointments. Commissioners serve until age seventy and hold the rank and retirement privileges of Judges of the Courts of First Instance.

Chapter II — Powers and Duties of the Commission

The Commission is empowered to issue certificates of public convenience and necessity; fix rates, tolls, and service standards; prescribe depreciation rates; approve issuance of capital stock and transfers of property, franchises, or certificates; investigate accidents and violations; and require uniform accounting systems and annual financial reports from public services under its jurisdiction.

Chapter III — Prohibitions

Public services may not operate without the proper certificate of public convenience; provide unsafe or inadequate service; grant undue preferences or engage in discriminatory treatment among customers; or issue free transportation or service except to specified categories, such as employees, charitable workers, or persons injured in accidents involving the operator.

Chapter IV — Penalties

Violations are punishable by fines of up to Two Hundred Pesos per day of violation, with corporate violations reaching up to Twenty-Five Thousand Pesos, and imprisonment of up to five years. Prosecution for certificate violations must commence within sixty days; other statutory violations, within one hundred eighty days.

Chapter VI — Fees

The Commission collects registration fees of Fifty Pesos, annual supervision fees based on capital stock at twenty centavos per One Hundred Pesos, and motor vehicle fees of Ten to Fifty Pesos annually depending on capacity.

Relation to NTC's Present Authority

Regulatory functions over telecommunications carriers under this Act were transferred to the National Telecommunications Commission upon its creation by EO 546 (1979). Section 19(b) of this Act — governing the fixing of rates and terms of service — is cited as part of the legal basis for NTC's interconnection and access-offer rules, including MC 10-07-2007.
