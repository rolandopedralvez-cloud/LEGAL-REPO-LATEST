Additional Guidelines for the Evaluation of New CATV Applications

Type
regulation
Status
active
Effective date
2018-08-31
Source/Authority
Act No. 3846, as amended; EO 546, s. 1979; EO 205, s. 1987; EO 436, s. 1997

Memorandum Circular No. 08-08-2018 supplements the NTC's evaluation criteria for new CATV (cable television) applications, updating minimum project-cost, household-income, and debt-to-equity standards, and setting a schedule for how many CATV operators may be authorized per service area based on the area's potential subscriber count.

Financial standards for new applications

The minimum project cost (including Head-End equipment) is set at PHP 5 million for service areas with fewer than 3,000 potential subscribers, and PHP 10 million for 3,000 or more. Average monthly household income thresholds for potential subscribers are set at PHP 35,000 for cities and PHP 20,000 for municipalities, and the maximum debt-to-equity ratio allowed for an applicant/operator is 70:30. Potential subscriber counts are computed using the latest Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) household-income data, with only the Circular's Annex needing updates as that data changes.

Renewal, over-building, and VAS registration

A CATV Provisional Authority holder seeking renewal must have rolled out operations within 18 months of the initial grant and invest in technology comparable to or better than the incumbent's; a Provisional Authority expired five years or more with no renewal motion filed is revoked. To address market over-building given competing DTH/DTU pay-TV services, only 60% of a service area's potential subscribers count toward the CATV subscriber total, regardless of how many DTH/DTU providers also serve that area. CATV operators are encouraged to also register as Value Added Service (VAS) providers as an incentive toward obtaining a Certificate of Authority.

Operators per service area

The number of CATV operators authorized per service area is tiered by potential subscriber count: one operator for 2,000 or fewer potential subscribers, two for 2,001-6,000, three for more than 6,000 up to 15,000, and multiple operators for more than 15,000 potential subscribers.

Effect on other issuances and effectivity

Any inconsistent circular, office order, or memorandum is deemed superseded or amended. The Circular takes effect fifteen (15) days after publication in a newspaper of general circulation and filing of three certified true copies with the UP Law Center.
