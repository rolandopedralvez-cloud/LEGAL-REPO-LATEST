Foundational Laws

Act No. 3396, as amended — An Act to Make the Installation of Radio Apparatus Obligatory for Ships of Philippine Register, Provide for the Operation Thereof and Establish Penalties for Violations

Status
Amended, still in force — foundational maritime radio law predating the Radio Control Law
Amended By
Act No. 4072; Republic Act No. 1539 (approved June 16, 1956)

Sections 1–2 — Mandatory Radio Equipment

It is unlawful for a covered Philippine-registered vessel to sail from any Philippine port without efficient radio apparatus for sending and receiving messages. Coverage applies to vessels engaged in coastwise trade authorized to carry passengers, and to cargo vessels of 350 gross tons or over on coastwise trade, regardless of crew size — except vessels confined to rivers, lakes, and bays, or short strait crossings of 60 miles or less lasting under five hours. Covered vessels must carry radio apparatus capable of sending and receiving over a distance of at least 200 nautical miles, day and night.

Sections 3–4 — Additional Equipment and Licensed Operators

The Secretary of Public Works and Communications may require additional sending plant capable of transmitting for at least four hours over a minimum distance of 200 miles, day and night. Radio apparatus must at all times be operated by an operator holding a first-class commercial radiotelegraph operator's license issued under the Radio Control Law and its implementing regulations.

Relation to Later Radio Law

This Act predates and is administered alongside Act No. 3846 (the Radio Control Law), which governs operator licensing generally; this Act's specific mandate is the shipboard-equipment requirement for Philippine-registered vessels.
