Presidential Decree No. 576-A — Regulating the Ownership and Operation of Radio and Television Stations and for Other Purposes

Status
Amended — franchise-termination clause (Sec. 6) has been superseded in practice by later administrative authority, but the ownership caps remain cited
Effective Date
November 11, 1974
Signed By
President Ferdinand E. Marcos, by Presidential Assistant Juan C. Tuvera

The preamble finds that radio is the chief vehicle for the dissemination of information to the public, that the airwaves are a limited public resource, and that measures are needed to prevent monopoly and concentration of ownership over broadcast facilities.

Section 1 — Capital Requirement

Every radio or television station must have sufficient capital to sustain at least one year of operations.

Section 2 — Public Service Programming

Every station must devote at least two hours a day, during prime time, to public service programming — defined to include news, public affairs, educational and cultural programs, and programs on scientific, technological, and governmental advances.

Section 3 — Ownership Limits

No person or entity may own or operate more than one radio or television station in a given municipality or city, and no person or entity may own or control, nationwide, more than five AM stations, five FM stations, and five television channels.

Section 4 — Divestiture of Excess Holdings

Persons or entities holding stations in excess of the limits set in Section 3 must divest the excess stations, through the Bureau of Telecommunications, within the period prescribed by the implementing rules.

Section 5 — Effect of Non-Compliance

Failure to comply with Sections 1 to 4 results in cancellation of the station's franchise or authorization and confiscation of its facilities and equipment in favor of the government.

Section 6 — Termination of Existing Franchises

All franchises to operate radio and television stations then existing are declared terminated as of December 31, 1981. Thereafter, continued operation requires reauthorization from the Board of Communications.

Section 7 — Penalties

Violation of this Decree is punishable by imprisonment of five months to six years, a fine of One Thousand to Ten Thousand Pesos, or both, at the discretion of the court.

Section 8 — Effectivity

This Decree takes effect immediately.

Later Legal Interpretation

A 1991 Department of Justice opinion traces the current authority of NTC to authorize broadcast operation, without requiring a prior legislative franchise, through this Decree's termination of existing franchises (Section 6) and the Board of Communications'/Secretary of Public Works and Communications' resulting authorization power — predecessor authority to that later vested in NTC by EO 546.
